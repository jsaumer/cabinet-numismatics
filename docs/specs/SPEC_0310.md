# SPEC_0310: Cabinet v0.31.0, bars and rounds (P11)

Status: **approved by the owner on 22 September 2026** (the two open questions
answered below; built stage by stage on `p11-bullion`, PR 22). Decisions of
22 September 2026:
bars and rounds become a third item type (option C of the research below),
fully featured, as **v0.31.0**; single sign-on (P8 A2) moves to **v0.32.0**
(and on 23 September 2026 to v0.33.0, behind the share view, SPEC_0320).
Built after v0.30.0 is tagged, on `p11-bullion` (PR 22), rebased onto `main`
once PR 21 merges.

## Why

The owner asked how to add a piece to the bullion stack and found nothing
under Add. The stack (v0.28.0) is worked out from ordinary items (owned, a
precious metal named in the composition, a weight, a fineness), so a bar
had to be entered as a Coin with a required denomination, where it then
counted as a coin everywhere. Looking further turned up two bugs in how the
metal and fineness are read, and three smaller gaps.

## What the research found

- **Numista files bars and rounds as exonumia, "Bullion › Bar"** (for
  example a 1 oz Singapore Mint and PAMP bar, `exonumia430821`), with a
  country as issuer and a title like "1 Oz Silver - Majestic Merlion".
  Sovereign bullion coins (Eagles, Maples) are coins.
- **Stack trackers** (Stack Tracker, BullionLog, MetalMetric, Gold Tracker)
  record a form (coin, bar, round), metal, weight, purity, the mint or
  refiner, a serial for bars, and the premium over spot at purchase.
- **Cabinet cannot reach Numista's bars today**: "Fill from Numista"
  searches only `coin` and `banknote` (`routers/catalogue.py`), and the
  Numista account import skips exonumia (`import_formats.py`).
- **A new type value needs no migration**: `items.type` is an SQLAlchemy
  `Enum("coin", "note", native_enum=False)`, a `VARCHAR(10)` with no database
  constraint (migration `0002`), so the Python enum in `models/item.py` gains
  the value alongside `schemas.ItemTypeName` and the table is untouched.
  `serial_number` and
  `issuer` already exist (shown for notes only); `shape` exists for coins.

Options weighed: A, a Coin preset only (bars still count as coins, no
serial or refiner, Numista's bars unreachable); B, a form column on coins
(a migration, and still "coins" with a meaningless denomination); **C, a
third type (chosen)**; D, a general exonumia type (tokens and medals too,
which the owner doesn't collect).

## Scope

**In scope**

1. **Metal detection fixed (a bug).** `pricing.detect_metal` matches the
   word anywhere, so these are wrongly precious today (checked against the
   code): "Nickel silver" and "German silver" (silver), "Nordic gold", the
   euro 10, 20, and 50 cent alloy (gold), "Gold plated brass" (gold),
   "Silver-plated copper" (silver). Effects: euro cents under Gold in the
   metal breakdown, the Stack page's "no weight or fineness" count inflated,
   melt coverage asking for a fineness, and the spot-at-purchase field on the
   form. New rule, in one function:
   - whole words only (`golden` is not gold);
   - the named alloys `nickel silver`, `german silver`, and `nordic gold`
     are not precious;
   - a metal followed by `plated`, `washed`, or `gilt`/`gilded` (with or
     without a hyphen) is a surface, not the metal, so "Gold plated silver"
     is silver and "Silver-gilt" is silver; `clad` is **not** stripped (40%
     silver-clad halves hold silver);
   - with two metals left (a bimetallic gold centre and silver ring), the
     one with the larger attached percentage wins, else the first named,
     not the dictionary order used today.
2. **Fineness parsing fixed (a bug).** "Copper 10%, Silver 90%" gives 0.100
   today, because the first percentage wins, in both parsers. There are two
   (`pricing.effective_fineness`, percentages only, and
   `numista.fineness_from_composition`, which also reads `.925` and
   `999.9`). One parser, `pricing.parse_fineness(text, metal)`, used by
   both: the percentage attached to the detected metal ("90% silver" or
   "silver 90%"), then a decimal (`.925`), then millesimal (`925`,
   `999.9`), then named standards: sterling .925, coin silver .900,
   Britannia .958, and gold carats (24K .999, 22K .9167, 18K .750,
   14K .585, 9K .375). Nothing found stays `None`; nothing is guessed.
3. **A third item type, `bullion`**, shown as "Bar or round":
   - **Schema**: `ItemTypeName` gains `bullion`; no migration. Country and
     denomination stay required (the refiner's country, as Numista has it;
     the denomination holds the product name, "1 oz silver bar", suggested
     by the form). The year-or-ND rule does not apply to bullion: a bar with
     no year has neither, and `year_label` is empty for it (the label drops
     the part rather than printing "ND").
   - **Label**: `issuer or country`, then denomination, then the year if
     any: "PAMP Suisse 1 oz silver bar".
   - **Fields**: composition, weight, fineness, shape, diameter/size,
     serial number, issuer (shown as "Refiner or mint"), grade and cert
     (NGC slabs some rounds), the usual acquisition and storage fields.
     Strike, designations, die axis, the date as struck, and the note-only
     fields are hidden. Fancy serial traits are not computed for bullion (a
     bar's serial is not a "radar"): `_build_item` and `_sync_derived` skip
     it, and a type change to bullion clears them.
   - **Counts**: `/api/stats/collection` gains `counts.bullion`; `coins`
     and `notes` keep their meaning, so the Homepage tile keeps working
     (docs/monitoring.md shows the optional third field). The type
     breakdown and `cabinet_items{type}` gain the value. The dashboard's
     value hero reads "12 coins · 3 notes · 4 bars and rounds" (the last only
     when non-zero).
   - **Pricing**: melt and the stack need nothing new. PCGS stays coins
     only. Numista prices whatever its catalogue prices; a bar with none is
     the usual "can't price" answer.
   - **Numista**: the fill searches `category=exonumia` when the form's type
     is bullion, and `catalogue_fields` maps an exonumia type whose
     sub-type reads as bullion (bar, round, ingot) to `bullion`; any other
     exonumia is refused with a message. The account import and the export
     file import take bullion exonumia the same way and still skip tokens
     and medals. The field holding the sub-type was unconfirmed when this
     was written; the owner's probe on 22 September 2026 settled it
     (`object_type`, see the build log), so nothing here waits on the road
     to v1.0.0.
   - **Imports**: a spreadsheet Type cell reading `bar`, `round`, `ingot`,
     or `bullion` maps to it; the Cabinet CSV round-trips it (the product
     name stays in the `denomination` column, so the export is exact).
   - **The list**: the type filter gains "Bars and rounds"; bulk edit can
     set it.
4. **Discoverable entry.**
   - The Add form's type choice is Coin, Note, Bar or round.
   - For bullion, a **Metal** select (gold, silver, platinum, palladium)
     writes the composition, and fineness defaults to .999 (editable, .9999
     one click away); the denomination is suggested from weight, metal, and
     shape ("1 oz silver bar", "10 g gold bar", "1 oz silver round") until
     the owner types their own, the way `autoYear` works.
   - For a coin, a line under Composition: "Name a precious metal and give a
     weight and fineness to include it in the bullion stack."
   - The Stack page's empty state explains the same and links to Add with
     the type preset (`/items/new?type=bullion`).
5. **Weight in ounces.** A g / oz switch beside Weight on coins and bullion;
   ounces are troy, converted at 31.1035 g and stored in grams to four
   places. The item page shows grams and, for anything in the stack, fine
   ounces as today.
6. **The skipped pieces are listed.** `GET /api/stack` gains
   `skipped_items` (id, label, and what is missing: weight, fineness) next
   to the existing `skipped` count, and the Stack page lists them with links.
   `GET /api/items` gains `metal=` (`gold`, `silver`, `platinum`,
   `palladium`, or `none`), evaluated in Python with the shared detector
   like the metal breakdown (at 100 to 500 pieces, no column is needed).
7. **A value from the start.** `refresh_melt_estimates` today refreshes
   only pieces whose latest estimate is an old melt one, so a new bullion
   piece has no value until someone presses a button. It also takes owned
   pieces in the stack's terms with **no estimate at all** (never one whose
   latest is manual). And saving an item that qualifies adds a melt
   estimate **from the cached spot price only** when that is fresh, through
   `pricing.add_estimate`; no network call on the save path, as the stack
   rule already says.

**Out of scope:** a form column on coins (bullion coins stay Coin), tokens
and medals, a keyed source for purchase-day spot before March 2024 (a
roadmap candidate), five-nines fineness (`Numeric(5, 4)` stores .99999 as
1.0000, a 0.001% error, left alone), and assay-card photos as their own
designation (the "other" designation covers them).

## API changes (all additive)

| Where | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Item schemas | `type` accepts `bullion` |
| `GET /api/items` | `type=bullion`; new `metal=` |
| `GET /api/stats/collection` | `counts.bullion` |
| `GET /api/stats/breakdowns` | a `bullion` entry under type |
| `GET /api/stack` | `skipped_items` |
| `GET /api/numista/search` | `category` accepts `exonumia` |
| `/api/metrics` | `cabinet_items{type="bullion"}` |

No new route, so SPEC_0300's permission appendix and `tests/test_gate.py`
are untouched. An export holding bullion does not import into v0.30.0 or
older (the type is refused there); `api.md` says so.

## Stages

1. **Detection and fineness**: the shared detector and parser, `effective_fineness`
   and the Numista parser on top of them, the frontend `detectMetal` mirrored
   (the backend is the authority; a table of cases in the pytest suite, and a
   Playwright check that "Nickel silver" shows no spot field and "90% silver"
   does).
2. **The type, backend**: schemas, label, year rule, serial traits, counts,
   breakdown, metrics, the list filters, imports, Numista.
3. **The type, frontend**: the type choice, the bullion form, the product
   suggestion, g / oz, the coin hint, item hero and facts, the list, bulk
   edit, the dashboard hero and breakdown labels.
4. **Stack and melt**: `skipped_items`, the Stack page's list and empty state,
   melt for pieces with no estimate, melt on save from the cache.
5. **Proof and paperwork**: pytest for each stage, Playwright (add a bar, see
   it in the stack), the smoke script, a bar and a round in
   `seed_demo.py`, screenshots where the form changed, then README,
   api.md, data-model.md, monitoring.md, roadmap, implementation notes,
   frontend README, CLAUDE.md, the changelog, and version 0.31.0.

## The owner's answers (22 September 2026)

- The type's display name is **"Bar or round"** (the API value `bullion`; the
  list filter reads "Bars and rounds").
- The default fineness for a new bar or round is **.999**, editable.
